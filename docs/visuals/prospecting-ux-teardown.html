The finding that matters most

The market shows its work. You just hid yours.

Every source on lead scoring lands in the same place: reps don't trust a number they can't unpack. The pattern the tools converge on is a per-signal contribution list shown right next to the score.

Direct tension with feedback #10

Demandbase's guidance is blunt — if reps can't see why a lead scored 87, they won't use it. HubSpot shipped exactly this in 2025: which signals contributed most, per score.

VP title +15  ·  pricing page 3× this week +25
Bombora surge +20  ·  2 buying-committee members +15
ICP Tier 1 +12

That is structurally the same thing as your 0.85 × 25 = 21 — which your manager asked you to hide, and which we moved behind a footnote.

Both can be right. The industry writes contributions in plain points (+25), not as exposed arithmetic. Your manager objected to the multiplication, not to the transparency. Keep the explanation, drop the algebra.

Recommendations

Six changes, cheapest first

01

Rewrite the math as contributions

1 file

Same numbers, industry phrasing. This resolves the tension above — your manager gets the algebra gone, the market gets its glass box.

Specialty tier
0.90 × 35 = 31.5 → High-earning specialty
+32 of 35
02

Save the filters as named views

state already in the URL

Saved lists are the core object in Apollo, not a convenience. Your book filters already serialize to the URL — naming and storing them is the remaining step. "My Chicago derms", "Score 55+".

03

Lead with the trigger, not the specialty

copy change

Aidentified sells on event monitoring; you already detect the same class of events. Your card's second line spends itself on specialty and city — data the name already implies.

Dermatology · Chicago, IL → Licensed 2 months ago · bought a home
04

Put the outreach draft back

team decision

Catchlight leads with AI-written copy; Apollo ships sequences. Your deterministic templates still exist in git — 88 lines, no LLM. Your teammate removed them on purpose, so this is a conversation, not a commit.

05

Name the compliance layer

positioning

No competitor surfaces DNC exposure or routes every touch to a practice address. You do both, and it's currently styled as gray footnotes. In a regulated market that's the strongest thing you own.

06

Estimate the money

new data

Both advisor-native rivals lead with estimated investable assets. You infer wealth from specialty and property events but never state a figure. The honest version is a band with its basis shown, not a fabricated precise number.

On the evidence

What this is and isn't

Five web searches and three pages read. Worth knowing before you quote any of it:

  • Most results were comparison and marketing content, not primary UX teardowns. Nobody publishes annotated screenshots of these products.
  • The explainability finding is the best-supported — it recurs across independent sources and names a shipped HubSpot feature.
  • Feature claims come from vendor pages. Catchlight's "100k conversions" and Aidentified's "16B connections" are their own numbers.
  • I could not see any of these interfaces directly. Layout claims are inferred from descriptions.

If this feeds a slide, the safest citations are the explainability pattern and the named integrations. Treat scale figures as vendor-reported.
