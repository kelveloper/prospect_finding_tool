The Book — what a spread actually tells you

Six findings against the real first spread. The measurements below are the whole book, pulled from the API.

The finding everything else follows from

NoiseThree of the five columns are near-constant on every page

Counted across the first twelve entries — one spread, what an advisor actually sees at once:

ColumnOn spread 1Same value
Evidence11 × partial, 1 × strong92%
Fit band10 × strong, 2 × promising83%
Why now10 × new licence, 1 house, 1 move83%
Name & location12 distinct0%
Fit score12 distinct0%

And the band is constant by construction. Tier is assigned from standing — top 5% strong, next 15% promising — and the book is sorted by exactly that. So a page of a sorted list can only ever show one or two bands. The column is guaranteed to repeat itself before a single row is rendered.

Information is surprise. A column where eleven of twelve values match carries almost none, but it costs a column of width and twelve fixations per page. Three of them, and an advisor reads the word “partial” eleven times to learn nothing.

The rest

DuplicateThe band word restates the number beside it

strong 62.5 — the word is a function of the number’s rank, printed next to the number. Two encodings of one fact, twelve times a spread, in a column already sorted by it. Colour the figure instead and the word is free to go.

DuplicateThe avatars spell the name they sit beside

AB next to Ameen Barghi. Twelve filled circles, each carrying letters already printed two centimetres to the right, each pulling a fixation on the way down the page. They earn their place in the board’s card list, where a row is tall and sparse. A ledger line is neither.

DuplicateEvery column heading is printed twice

Specialty, Location, Why now, Evidence and Fit appear once per page, so ten headings and ten sort menus for five columns. Faithful to a printed ledger; twice the vocabulary for a reader, and two places to look for the same control.

MissingNothing shows which lines you have already worked

outreach_status is computed on the backend for exactly this, reaches the browser on every row, and is read by nothing. The backend’s own comment says it “powers the book-view status column”. There is no such column.

It matters more here than anywhere else in the product: the board shows one prospect, the book shows the list you are working down. Log a call, come back tomorrow, and the line looks identical to the two hundred you have not touched.

DecideThe book, still

A reviewer called the spread “a bit too much” and the page turn “even too much” — while liking the thing underneath it: twelve rows without scrolling. That judgement stands and this critique does not change it. What it adds: the two-column spread is the part doing work, and the ornament is separable from it whenever you decide.
