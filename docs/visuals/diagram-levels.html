Five Levels of the Same Diagram

Each one adds exactly one idea to the one before it. Start at the top and stop wherever the person you are talking to stops nodding.

Level 1

The whole thing in three boxes

The sentence version. Public records go in, a ranked list comes out.

Use when: someone asks in a corridor. Also the one you draw on a whiteboard.

Level 2

Name the sources, split the middle

Adds which four sources, and breaks the middle into the two things it actually does: work out who is who, then look at what changed.

Use when: your team lead asks how it works, and you have twenty seconds.

Level 3

Two kinds of record

Adds the idea that makes the rest make sense. Two sources describe a person; two describe an event. People get merged together. Events get attached to a person who already exists.

Use when: someone asks how you avoid mixing up two doctors with the same name.

Level 4

What gets thrown away

Adds the gates. Three points where a record is refused, and the rule behind all of them: a missed signal is safer than a wrong one.

Use when: the question is about trust, compliance, or false matches.

Level 5

How the number is made

Adds the seven signals and the 60 / 40 split. Real numbers, from the doctor at rank one.

Use when: someone asks “why is he number one?” or “where does 62 come from?”
